# Poke: a forced alarm on the phone

A **poke** makes a friend's phone ring like an alarm: loud, over the lock screen, until the person stops it, whatever their sound or notification settings say. This file is the one place that describes it: the rules (all platforms), what is built on Android, and the plan for iPhone (not built).

## The rules (the same on every platform, decided)
1. **A poke is a message sent with `poke: true`.** The sender taps the **🔔 Poke** button in the header of the friend's page (a separate function, on every page of the friend, not in the writing field). It opens an expandable frame under the header (`PokeFrame`, `friend-page/poke-frame.ts`) that says "This rings <name>'s phone like an alarm, even when it is silent" with **Cancel** and **Send poke**. The frame also says (`friendPage.pokeWaits`) that a poke waits up to 3 hours for a phone that is off or without connection and rings 30 seconds when it arrives (the texts say 3 hours and 30 seconds: change them with `POKE_TTL_MINUTES` and the default ring time). The frame also says, in one line, that for now a poke rings like an alarm on Android phones and in the Windows app (`friendPage.pokeNote`; change the line when the iPhone alarm is built). Send poke sends a normal message with the text 🔔 (locked like any message) and `poke: true`; it shows in both feeds.
2. **Only a friend who has a seat for me can poke me.** The seat is the consent: a person agrees to be poked by putting me on their list of 7; nothing is asked per poke, and no one who has not accepted me (a request, the waiting list) or who has me on the sleep list can poke. If I have the sender on my **sleep list**, their poke is ignored: the message arrives quietly like their other messages (no push, no sound, not stored as a poke). The server enforces it (`conversation.ts`: `poked` is false when the receiver's entry is `asleep`); the button is only shown for a confirmed friend who has a seat for me (`FriendContext.canPoke`: `status === 'confirmed'` and not `sleepsMe`); for a friend who has me on their sleep list the header already says so. A poke is only possible inside a confirmed friendship, like every message.
3. **No switch, only a pause for a limited time.** The receiver cannot turn pokes off for good: not the notification switches, not "No sound", not a setting for the friend. What they can do is **Pause pokes** (rule 7). (If this ever has to change, it is a product decision: the stores look closely at an app that can force a phone to ring, see "Store review" below.)
4. **It always says who.** The alarm shows the sender's name (`name · group` for a group) and the text of the message. A poke about another circle than the one the app is in still names the sender (an ordinary push for another circle only says "Activity in ...").
5. **It is short lived.** A poke that cannot be delivered within **`POKE_TTL_MINUTES` (default 180 = 3 hours, 1 to 1440; setting in `functions/.env`)** (phone off or offline) is dropped (the receiver's copy of the message also holds `pokeMinutes`, which the Windows app uses) (so a phone that was off for a while still rings when it is back, within 3 hours). **Once it is delivered it rings for 30 seconds** (a choice of 10 s, 30 s, 1, 2 or 5 minutes on each device, under **Settings → Notifications → "Poke settings"** (`notice-controls`, only in the Android app and the Windows app; `core/poke-settings.service.ts`). The page hands the choice to the app around it, which keeps it because the alarm runs when the page is closed: Android through `SevenFriendsAndroid.setPokeSeconds` / `pokeSeconds` (`NativeNotices.java`, kept in `PokeService`; the Android notification screen has the same choice under "Poke settings" and both always agree), Windows through a `pokeSeconds` message over the WebView2 bridge (`MainWindow.xaml.cs`, kept in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\SevenFriends\poke-seconds.txt`), or until Stop. The ring time can be made shorter or longer; the only way to stop pokes ringing is the time-limited pause (rule 7).
6. **Stop, or Open.** Stop silences it. Open silences it and opens that friend's page.

7. **Pause pokes** (decided with the owner): a person can stop their pokes from ringing for a limited time, **1 hour, 4 hours, until morning (08:00 on this computer's clock) or 24 hours; never longer than 24 hours (`POKE_PAUSE_MAX_MINUTES`)**. It is kept on the account (`pokePausedUntil`, a time; `PUT /profile/poke-pause { minutes }`, `{ minutes: null }` ends it, `GET /profile/poke-pause` -> `{ until }`; `profile.ts`, `poke-pause.ts`), so it holds on every device and in **every circle**. The server ends it by itself (it compares the time). While it lasts: (a) a poke to that person is **not a poke**: the message arrives as an ordinary message with the person's normal notification and sound, and is not stored as a poke (`conversation.ts`, `poked`); a poke that came during the pause never rings later; (b) their friends see the mood **"Silent"** (`MOOD_SILENT` = 5, icon `mood5.svg`) in every circle instead of their own mood, which is never changed and comes back when the pause is over (`friends.ts`, `withUnverified`); a friend's list shows the end of a pause the next time it is loaded (a pause that ends by time is not pushed to friends' apps); friends are never told the end time. (c) The sender's 🔔 frame says "{name} has paused pokes. A poke arrives as a normal message." (`friendPage.pokePaused`). **Where:** a card "Pokes are on / Pokes paused until 18:30" with Pause / Resume on **Home** under the mood, and in Settings → Notifications → Poke settings (`poke-pause/`, `core/poke-pause.service.ts`; the card of the Home mood row shows "Silent" for me too). Tests: `functions/test/poke-pause.test.ts`.

## What the server sends (`functions/src/push.ts`)
`sendPush(uid, { ..., poke: true })`:
- It is sent whatever `noticePreferences` and the sound choice say (`!push.force && !push.poke && !notices[kind]`), and never `silent`.
- The data carries `poke: "1"`, `title` (the sender), `body` (the text; if the receiver chose "hide the text on the lock screen" it is "New message", as for any message: that setting still holds, the sender's name does not hide), `url` (the friend's page), `id`, `circle`.
- **Android:** no `notification` block, only the data, `priority: "high"`, `ttl` = `POKE_TTL_MINUTES` (default 180). Only a data message runs the app's own code while the app is closed.
- **Web and iPhone** today: the ordinary push (alert with sound), also with the same expiry (the same `ttl` is used for `apns-expiration`).

## Android (built)
Files in `client-wrappers/android/app/src/main/`:
- `PokeMessagingService.java` extends the Capacitor push plugin's `MessagingService`; for `poke=1` it starts `PokeService`, everything else goes to the plugin. The manifest removes the plugin's own service (`tools:node="remove"`). `app/build.gradle` adds `firebase-messaging` (the plugin keeps it private).
- `PokeService.java`: a foreground service (`mediaPlayback`) that loops the phone's alarm sound with `USAGE_ALARM`, raises the alarm volume to the maximum and puts it back, vibrates, and shows a high-priority notification (channel `poke_alarm`, `setBypassDnd(true)`, category alarm) with a full-screen intent and a Stop button. It stops after the ring time (30 seconds by default, a choice on the phone) and tells the screen to close (`POKE_DONE` broadcast).
- `PokeActivity.java`: shown over the lock screen, turns the screen on; the sender's name big, the text, **Stop** and **Open**.
- Manifest: `FOREGROUND_SERVICE`, `FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MEDIA_PLAYBACK`, `USE_FULL_SCREEN_INTENT`, `VIBRATE`. Texts in `res/values*/strings.xml` (`poke_*`: English, Norwegian, French, Spanish).
- Known limits: from Android 14 a full-screen screen over the lock screen may need the person to allow it in the app's settings (the sound and the notification still work); Do Not Disturb is only bypassed when the person has given Do Not Disturb access; phone makers' battery savers can delay a push (the person may have to exempt the app).
- Not tested on a phone yet.

## Windows (built, compiles, not tried on a PC yet)
The Windows app (`client-wrappers/windows`) has no push service (WNS is not done), so a poke reaches it through the live connection of the web page, which keeps running while the window is **hidden in the tray**. So it rings **only while the app is running** (not when it was quit from the tray, or the computer is off or asleep).
- `realtime.service.ts` (`tellWindowsAppAboutPokes`): a new message with `poke: true` (not older than `pokeMinutes` of the message, default 180; not the first snapshot) is sent to the app with `window.chrome.webview.postMessage({ type: 'poke', id, name, text, url })`, only in the Windows app (`SevenFriendsWindows` in the user agent).
- `MainWindow.xaml.cs` (`WebMessageReceived`): accepts it only from the app's own pages and a path that starts with `/`, and opens `PokeWindow`.
- `PokeWindow.cs`: a window over everything (`Topmost`), the sender's name big, the text, **Stop** and **Open**; the Windows alert sound repeats every 1.4 s until Stop, Open, or the ring time (30 seconds unless changed in Settings). A second poke while it rings updates the same window. Open brings the main window forward on that friend's page.
- **The main window comes to the front** for a new message that makes a sound and for a poke (`MainWindow.BringToFront`: the window is made topmost for a moment, because Windows does not let a program take the front otherwise; it also comes back from the tray). The page asks for it with `{ type: 'front' }` over the bridge (`RealtimeService.bringWindowsAppToFront`), so a friend on the sleep list does not bring it to the front. Windows can still refuse to take the focus from the window in use; then the taskbar icon flashes.
- Windows has no silent switch to get past; the sound follows the volume of the computer (it is not raised). A computer that is muted does not ring.
- Later, with WNS and an MSIX app: a poke as a toast with `scenario="alarm"` for the time the app is not running.

## Browser (built, not tried)
A browser tab or an installed web app is a device of the person too. When a poke arrives through the live connection in a browser page, `PokeAlarmService` / `poke-alarm/` show a **band at the top of the page** ("Poke from <name>", the text, Stop, Open) and repeat an alarm tone until Stop, Open or 30 seconds; the web push notification of a poke stays on the screen until it is clicked (`requireInteraction` in `firebase-messaging-sw.js`) and vibrates. A browser may keep the tone silent until the person has touched the page once.
**One PC with both the Windows app and a browser:** the server cannot tell which browser device is on the same PC as the Windows app (a browser registers only a push token and its own name, the Windows app registers no token), so a browser device is NOT removed when the Windows app is installed. Both ring: the Windows window (`PokeWindow`) and the browser band. To ring in one place only, close the browser tab or switch off notifications in that browser (Settings → Notifications).

## iPhone (prepared in code, not built or tried: needs a Mac and Apple)
**The hard fact:** an iPhone app cannot ring over the silent switch or a Focus by itself, and a push cannot run code that plays a looping sound. The only way for a REMOTE poke is a **Critical Alert**. Everything that can be done without a Mac and without Apple's approval is written; the rest is a short list for the owner.

**What is in the code now**
- `functions/src/push.ts` (`deliver`): a poke (`alarm`) is sent to the iPhone as `sound: { critical: true, name: "poke.caf", volume: 1 }` and `interruption-level: critical`. A paused person gets an ordinary push (no `alarm`). Until Apple has approved the entitlement and the person has said yes, iOS plays it as an ordinary notification: nothing breaks. Test: `poke-pause.test.ts`.
- `client-wrappers/ios/App/App/poke.caf`: the sound, 25 s of an alarm-clock pattern (a critical alert plays a file ONCE, at most 30 s, so the pattern repeats inside the file). Made by `npm run poke:sound` (`scripts/make-poke-sound.mjs`, runs on any computer); already in the Xcode project.
- `client-wrappers/ios/App/App/PokeAlerts.swift`: a small Capacitor plugin `PokeAlerts.requestCritical()` and `MainViewController` (used by `SceneDelegate`), listed in the Xcode project. The web app calls it just before it turns push on (`core/push.service.ts`): **the first permission question must include critical alerts** (the option cannot be added by a later question, and the Capacitor push plugin asks without it), so this plugin asks first; the push plugin then finds the answer given.
- `core/realtime.service.ts`: when the iPhone app is OPEN, a poke also shows the band at the top of the page (`PokeAlarmService`), because iOS shows a push in the foreground only if the app lets it.

**What the owner must do (in this order)**
1. **Apply for the Critical Alerts entitlement** on Apple's form (https://developer.apple.com/contact/request/notifications-critical-alerts-entitlement/), as the account holder, for the bundle id `io.sevenfriends.app`. Say what it is for: a friend (one of 7 the person chose) can ring a person's phone for something urgent, the person can pause it for up to 24 hours or put a friend on the sleep list, and it rings for 30 seconds at most. This can take days to weeks; nothing below works without it.
2. When approved: in the Apple developer site, add the capability to the App ID and make a new provisioning profile; in Xcode (Signing & Capabilities) add the entitlement `com.apple.developer.usernotifications.critical-alerts` = `true` to `App.entitlements`. **Do not add that line before Apple has approved it: the app will not sign.**
3. On a Mac: finish the steps in `client-wrappers/ios/README.md` (FirebaseMessaging package, `GoogleService-Info.plist`, the APNs key, the Team) and run the app on a real iPhone (a simulator cannot test push).
4. Test on the iPhone with the silent switch on and a Focus on, screen locked: poke it from a friend who has a seat for it. In Settings → Seven Friends → Notifications, **Critical Alerts** must be on.
5. Decide on repeating (below) after step 4.

**Not decided / later:** a poke rings once, up to 25 s. To make it harder to miss the backend may send the same poke **again after 30 and 60 seconds** (same `apns-collapse-id` = the poke's message id, so the phone shows one notification) until the message is read, but it must stop when the person opens the chat on any device. The iPhone has no setting for the ring time (the file is fixed).

**Fall-backs if Apple says no:** a **Time Sensitive** notification (`interruption-level: time-sensitive`, entitlement granted without review) breaks through Focus but NOT the silent switch. AlarmKit (iOS 26) rings through Silent and Focus but is scheduled on the device, so a friend's push cannot start it. CallKit/PushKit (the poke as a phone call) is refused by Apple for anything that is not a call.

## Store review
Both stores treat "another person can force my phone to ring" as a risk. Be ready to explain: pokes only come from a confirmed friend (one of 7), the receiver can pause pokes (up to 24 hours) or put a friend on the sleep list, there is a hard limit on how long it rings, and the person is told about it when the app asks for the permission.
